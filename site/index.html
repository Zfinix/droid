Connect a coding agent in three steps

  1. In Droid, turn on Connect a computer. Turn it off whenever you want the phone back to yourself.

  2. Plug the phone into your computer with USB debugging on, then pair:

    npx -y @withaster/droid pair --adb

  3. Add Droid to Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Zed, JetBrains and VS Code:

    npx -y @withaster/droid setup all

Needs Node 20 or later. Wi-Fi and Tailscale work too. CLI guide ↗

Privacy and safety

What it can access.

The screen, through Accessibility

Droid reads the screen and taps through Android's Accessibility service, once you turn on Screen access. No root.

What goes to the model

Your messages, the screen text and the screenshots the agent asks for go to the model you pick. A model on your own computer keeps them on your network. The free models keep logs.

Keys stay on the phone

Model keys and bot tokens are stored on the phone and sent only to the service they belong to. Setup links carry them in the part of the link that never reaches this site.

It asks, and it stops

It asks in chat before it sends messages, pays or calls, on every channel. The Stop button in its notification stops it at once.

Computers you connect

Agents connected over MCP drive the screen directly. They can only connect while Connect a computer is on, and you can turn it off at any time.

Open source

The app and the agent are Apache-2.0, so anyone can read what they do. Source on GitHub ↗

Droid is experimental. Read the privacy page for exactly what goes where.

Support

Questions, answered.

Is it really free?

Yes. The app and the agent are Apache-2.0. The free models need no account or key, but they run on a trial service that keeps logs. You only pay a model provider if you pick a paid one. Aster Relay, coming soon, will be an optional $5 a month.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 8 or later on a 64-bit (arm64) phone, which covers most phones from the last several years. It does not run on iPhone, but you can message it from an iPhone over iMessage through Photon. Droid is experimental, so expect rough edges.

Android or Play Protect warns me when I install it

Droid comes from GitHub, not the Play Store, so Android asks first. Allow "Install unknown apps" for your browser when it asks. If Play Protect warns, tap "More details", then "Install anyway".

The Screen access switch is greyed out

Android 13 and later restricts this for apps installed outside the Play Store. Tap the switch once, then open App info for Droid, tap the three-dot menu, choose "Allow restricted settings", and go back to turn it on.

Do I need to root my phone?

No. Droid reads the screen and taps through Android's Accessibility service.

What does the model see?

Your messages, the screen text and the screenshots the agent asks for. With a model on your own computer, such as Ollama or LM Studio, that stays on your network. With a cloud model, it goes to that provider. The free models run on a trial service that keeps logs, so keep banking, health and other private screens away from them.

Will it spend my money or message people?

Not without asking. Before it sends a message, pays for something or makes a call, it asks in chat, on every channel. The Stop button in its notification stops it at any time. Agents you connect over MCP, such as Claude Code, drive the screen directly; they can only connect while Connect a computer is on in the app.

Where do WhatsApp and iMessage come from?

From Photon, which connects Droid to WhatsApp and iMessage. You need a Photon account and its project key. Telegram only needs a bot token from @BotFather, and the chat in the app needs nothing.

How do I report a security problem?

Privately, through a GitHub security advisory. For anything else, open an issue.
